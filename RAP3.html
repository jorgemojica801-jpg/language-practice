Syntactic Blueprint

Automotive Grammar in Practice

Master structural rules for troubleshooting, technical comparisons, safety directives, and scheduling repairs.

Rule 1 Zero Conditional (Cause & Effect)

Expresses mechanical facts, universal safety laws, and immediate physical consequences.

IF / WHEN + [Present Simple Clause] , [Present Simple Clause]
Correct Mechanical Rule: If coolant drops below the limit, the engine overheats.
Incorrect Form: If coolant will drop, engine will overheat.

Rule 2 Technical Recommendation & Safety

Differentiates advice, urgent warnings, absolute obligations, and potential alternatives.

Had better Strong warning of negative consequences "You had better disconnect the power."
Should Standard recommendation/advice "We should replace the air filter."
Must Mandatory safety obligation "Technicians must wear safety goggles."
Could Feasible optional alternative "We could order spare parts online."

Rule 3 Scheduled Arrangements + Time Prepositions

Uses Present Continuous for fixed maintenance agendas paired with exact prepositions (at time, on day, in month/year).

Now (Decision)
"We are installing the lathe ON Monday AT 8:00 AM"
AT Specific Times at 7:30 AM / at noon
ON Days & Dates on Tuesday / on Oct 22
IN Months, Years, Shifts in October / in 2026

Rule 4 Equipment & Solution Comparison

Grading performance, durability, and operational costs across tools and workshop machinery.

Comparative (2 Options) [Adj+er than / More adj than]

"Synthetic oil is more efficient than conventional oil."

Superlative (1 vs All) [The adj+est / The most adj]

"This OBD-II scanner is the most reliable diagnostic tool in the workshop."
